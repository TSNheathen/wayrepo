# Evidence inventory for the production-complete Akros cutline

Research support for the Wayfinder ticket **[Define the production-complete Akros product cutline](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/6)**. This note inventories evidence and conflicts; it does **not** choose `launch`, `later`, `archive/read-only`, or `retire` classifications.

All filesystem paths are relative to the `wayrepo` root. The adjacent `../context` tree is the local evidence base.

## Provenance and decision state

- The Akros package is a Production Deployment Snapshot captured about five days before the map and confirmed live and in use. It proves deployed code, routes, customizations, and Connector seams, but not database-controlled enablement, traffic, schedules, or operator use. The Wayfinder map makes that distinction explicit and says runtime-system discovery does not block the product cutline ([map](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/1); `docs/research/akros-production-baseline.md:5-15`).
- **[Reconstruct the Akros production product and operating baseline](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/2)** resolved that Akros is a thin deployment over WRShop with rich B2C/B2B-shaped commerce, authenticated-portal, aftercare, CMS, and ABRA-shaped surfaces, subject to the deployment-versus-enablement distinction ([resolution comment](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/2#issuecomment-5380984270)).
- **[Extract legacy commerce invariants and lessons for Akros](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/3)** resolved that durable commerce knowledge and characterization cases—not the old runtime architecture or the generated 43-feature portfolio—are the relevant legacy handoff ([resolution comment](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/3#issuecomment-5380984497)). The feature catalog itself says it is static-code interpretation rather than runtime proof and that unresolved hypotheses must not be committed commercially (`../context/stare_jadro_featury/output/README_PRODUCT_CATALOG.md:5-27,97-110`).
- **[Assess Ontos, ERP overlap, and prior commerce attempts against Akros](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/4)** resolved that OntOS is a candidate kernel, not a commerce engine or production-hardened platform; ERP overlap is narrow and non-gating; and conflicting historical commerce proposals do not select the target ([resolution comment](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/4#issuecomment-5380984707)).
- The later correction on **[Capture runtime facts needed for integration and migration](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/5)** supersedes any “historical archive” reading of the package and requires discovery of every active External Business System rather than assuming ABRA is active ([correction](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/5#issuecomment-5381095455)). Its sequencing decision places selective runtime capture after this cutline and domain work ([sequencing](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/5#issuecomment-5381125383)).

## Legal, continuity, and acceptance evidence

The local evidence establishes customer-facing mechanisms, not a current legal opinion or compliance sign-off:

- Checkout requires terms/privacy confirmation and has separate optional consent slots for marketing, Heureka, Zboží, and NajNákup; authenticated portal history renders consent identity, type, and acceptance timestamp (`../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/project/templates/content/basket/block.submit.tpl:130-148`; `../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/project/templates/content/user/block.homepage.tpl:39-62`). Newsletter and claim forms also include consent components (`../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/project/templates/components/form.newsletter.tpl:1-12`; `../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/project/templates/components/form.claim.simple.tpl:61-70`).
- Product and checkout templates expose an 18+ marker/confirmation, while product presentation includes VAT-inclusive/exclusive prices, recycling/copyright fees, and delivery surcharges (`../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/project/readme.html:180-203`; `../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/project/templates/content/basket/block.submit.tpl:3-8`).
- An EET receipt block remains in order detail (`../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/project/templates/content/user/block.order_detail.tpl:128-154`). Its presence is historical/deployed code evidence, not proof that it is enabled or still legally required.
- URL identity and acquisition continuity are material: the deployment exposes sitemap, robots, feed, HTML rewrite, CMS, and catch-all route shapes (`../context/team-1633516729428-akros-124b361ce570/public_html/.htaccess:74-92`), while product/category templates emit canonical, breadcrumb, and structured commerce data (`docs/research/akros-production-baseline.md:218-220`).
- Migration evidence includes tens of thousands of customer/external-ID mappings and price-type updates, plus malformed-email/encoding cleanup evidence; the files contain PII and must remain controlled (`docs/research/akros-production-baseline.md:191-204`). Historical continuity may span product/content/media/URLs, mappings, catalog/commercial state, customers/company hierarchies/consents, baskets/orders/invoices/claims, and CMS/legal/settings data (`docs/research/akros-production-baseline.md:206-216`).
- The package has no Akros behavioral or end-to-end suite; files named `*Test.php` are operational/debug scripts. Therefore handoff BDD scenarios are candidate acceptance descriptions, not executable proof (`docs/research/akros-production-baseline.md:222-228`; `../context/stare_jadro_featury/output/08-basket-and-checkout-core.md:42-79`).
- A production acceptance baseline still lacks observed availability/performance/security posture, payment/email reliability, monitoring/alerting, backups/restore, incident and release evidence, accessibility evidence, and accountable operating owners (`docs/research/akros-production-baseline.md:230-255`).

No local artifact establishes the current Czech/EU obligation set, approved terms/privacy wording, retention periods, cookie-consent policy, accessibility target, or counsel/data-owner acceptance. Those are product/legal inputs, not facts safely inferred from old templates.

## Directly relevant accepted OntOS decisions

Only three ADRs are unconditionally `Accepted`; their scope is technical foundation, not the Akros product promise:

- Operational state is canonical in Postgres; Neo4j is optional and replayable, and operational decisions must not depend on it (`../context/ontos/adr/0004-postgres-canonical-neo4j-projection.md:1-19`).
- Authentication, relationship authorization, and business policy are separate responsibilities: BetterAuth, SpiceDB, and the OntOS Policy Layer respectively (`../context/ontos/adr/0005-betterauth-spicedb-policy-layer.md:1-19`).
- A BetterAuth session alone is not an OntOS login; it must resolve through an active Principal binding, Principal, and Tenant (`../context/ontos/adr/0014-authenticated-principal-session.md:1-5`). This decides the OntOS staff/session contract, not whether storefront retail or B2B customer identity uses that topology.

Explicit domain tables plus `ResourceRef` and “no product AI” are only `Accepted for current planning`; unified MicroVerticals, modular monolith, Action-driven writes, module state, outbox workers, and related choices remain `Proposed` (`docs/research/ontos-erp-commerce-assessment.md:60-72`). Current OntOS implements a real kernel/Ticketing slice but none of catalog, pricing, cart/checkout, inventory reservation, order, payment/refund, fulfillment, tax, storefront, or Commerce Admin (`docs/research/ontos-erp-commerce-assessment.md:74-101`). None of these artifacts can supply or classify Akros product capabilities.

## Evidence gaps that can materially change the cutline

1. **Channel promise:** whether both retail/B2C and trade/B2B must launch, including anonymous access and the required parent/subordinate/approval journeys.
2. **Commercial promises:** the customer-visible truth for assortment, price/discount precedence, quantities/packages, availability/backorder, tax/currency/country behavior, minimum order, credit/saldo, complete-order-only, delivery date, and order-change policy.
3. **Aftercare and history:** required self-service and operator outcomes for order status, shipment, invoices/credit notes, cancellation/return/refund, claims, repeat order, and which historical records must be migrated versus retained read-only.
4. **Operating completeness:** the real Akros roles and recurring happy/exception paths for catalog/content, customers/B2B, orders, payments, shipping, claims, feeds/SEO, and failed Connector work.
5. **Legal promise:** authoritative current terms/privacy/consent, age-restricted-sale, retention, invoicing/tax, cookie/marketing, and accessibility requirements plus their acceptance owners.
6. **Acceptance bar:** explicit critical journeys and non-functional targets for correctness, security/privacy, accessibility, performance/availability, SEO/URL continuity, observability, backup/restore, data reconciliation, and operational sign-off.

These are cutline-level gaps because they can change the promised product. Exact provider credentials, schedules, field mappings, payloads, volumes, job telemetry, and detailed System-of-Record allocation remain important follow-on integration/migration facts, but the map explicitly says they should not delay the product cutline unless one changes a customer-visible promise ([map](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/1); [runtime sequencing](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/5#issuecomment-5381125383)).

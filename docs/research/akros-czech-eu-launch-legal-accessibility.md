# Czech/EU legal and accessibility inputs to the Akros launch cutline

Research support for **[Define the production-complete Akros product cutline](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/6)**, checked on **2026-08-22**. This is a product-planning note, not legal advice. It does not establish Akros's seller identity, VAT status, company size, catalogue, data practices, or approved legal wording; accountable Akros owners must confirm those facts.

## Applicability boundary

- Czech consumer-contract rules below apply when a natural person buys outside their trade or profession. They should govern the accepted B2C journey, not be copied blindly into company purchasing. B2B terms, remedies, authority, and invoicing need their own approved contract policy.
- GDPR applies to personal data, including data about identifiable Principals acting for a Counterparty. Czech cookie rules apply to storing or reading information on a user's device. Czech electronic-marketing rules do not distinguish between natural- and legal-person recipients, according to the Czech data-protection authority ([GDPR, Articles 4–6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng); [ÚOOÚ marketing FAQ, question 25](https://uoou.gov.cz/cinnost/obchodni-sdeleni/casto-kladene-otazky-k-zakonu-c-4802004-sb)).
- The Czech accessibility act covers specified services, including consumer e-commerce. It does not make every internal B2B/admin surface an EAA service, but shared surfaces used to conclude a consumer contract are within the launch applicability assessment ([Act 424/2023 Sb., § 2](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2023/424); [Directive (EU) 2019/882, Articles 2–3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng)).

## Consumer offer, price, and checkout

For a Czech distance consumer sale, the storefront must provide clear pre-contract information in time for the decision. At product-cutline level this includes at least seller/contact identity, the goods' main characteristics, total price including taxes and applicable charges (or how uncalculable charges are determined), payment and delivery/performance terms, complaint handling, duration/termination where relevant, withdrawal conditions and model form, and the applicable out-of-court dispute route. The order-confirmation step must prominently repeat the information that determines what is bought and paid; a button or equivalent control must unambiguously say that ordering creates an obligation to pay. Preselected paid extras are not valid consent. The consumer must receive contract confirmation on a durable medium ([current Czech Civil Code, §§ 1811 and 1820–1827](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2012/89); [Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, Articles 6 and 8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/2022-05-28); [ČOI order-button guidance](https://coi.gov.cz/tlacitko/)).

If a consumer price is personalized through automated decision-making, that fact must also be disclosed before the transaction. If consumer reviews are ever surfaced, the seller must explain whether and how their origin is checked and must not claim verified purchase/use without reasonable and proportionate verification. These are conditional requirements: neither source proves that Akros uses personalized pricing or will launch reviews ([Czech Civil Code, § 1820](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2012/89); [ČOI review guidance](https://coi.gov.cz/recenze/)).

When a price reduction for a consumer product is announced, the displayed reference is generally the lowest price at which that product was offered and sold during the preceding 30 days; Czech law has specific rules for newly offered/perishable products and uninterrupted progressively increasing discounts. Quantity offers and genuinely individualized reductions are treated differently. This requires price-history facts and a promotion type, not merely a crossed-out "list price" ([Consumer Protection Act 634/1992 Sb., § 12a](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/1992/634); [ČOI discount guidance](https://coi.gov.cz/slevy/)).

For consumer products within the General Product Safety Regulation's scope, an online offer has had to show clearly and visibly, since **13 December 2024**, the manufacturer's name and postal/electronic address; the EU responsible person's equivalent details when the manufacturer is outside the EU; product identification including a picture and type/identifier; and required warnings or safety information in a language consumers can readily understand. Sector-specific product law may add or supersede requirements ([Regulation (EU) 2023/988, Articles 2, 4 and 19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj/eng)).

**Launch acceptance implications**

- Separate a consumer checkout policy from B2B ordering. Test the final B2C step for prominent item, quantity/configuration, full price/charges, delivery/payment facts, and an unambiguous payment-obligation action.
- Snapshot the accepted product, seller, price, tax, fee, delivery, terms/policy versions, and legally relevant acknowledgements, then deliver confirmation in a durable form.
- Store the price history and promotion classification needed to calculate consumer discount references. Do not apply the 30-day algorithm indiscriminately to negotiated company pricing.
- Keep personalized-price and review-verification disclosures behind explicit capability flags; apply their acceptance tests if those capabilities are picked up from `later`.
- Make the product-content model carry safety economic-operator, identifier, warning, and language data; block publication to a covered consumer channel when required data are absent.
- Keep Czech ADR information current. Do **not** restore an old EU ODR-platform link: Regulation (EU) 2024/3228 repealed that platform regime from **20 July 2025**, while Czech ADR obligations continue ([EU repeal](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/3228/oj/eng); [ČOI ADR duties](https://coi.gov.cz/pro-podnikatele/informace-pro-prodejce-zbozi-a-sluzeb/mimosoudni-reseni-spotrebitelskych-sporu-adr/)).

## Withdrawal, returns, defects, and complaints

A consumer generally has 14 days to withdraw from a distance goods contract, counted from possession under the statutory delivery cases. Failure to give the required withdrawal information can extend the period by up to 12 months. The consumer can use the model form or any unequivocal statement. Both the return and reimbursement flows have 14-day rules, subject to the seller's ability to withhold reimbursement until goods or dispatch evidence arrive and to statutory allocations of delivery/return cost. Statutory exceptions exist—for example certain made-to-order, rapidly perishable, unsealed hygiene, and fully performed/delivered digital cases—but must be matched to the actual product or service rather than implemented as one blanket exclusion ([Czech Civil Code, §§ 1829–1837](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2012/89); [Consumer Rights Directive, Articles 9–16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/2022-05-28); [ČOI distance-purchase guidance](https://coi.gov.cz/faq/a-nakup-pres-internet-2/)).

For consumer goods, the Czech regime is liability for a defect present on receipt and manifested within two years, not a blanket two-year quality guarantee; the first year carries the consumer-favouring presumption described by ČOI. A complaint must be accepted through the required channels, documented in writing with prescribed facts, decided/handled without undue delay, and completed within 30 days unless the consumer agrees to longer. Current Czech rules also require the complaint record to support the period during which the consumer could not use the goods ([Czech Civil Code, §§ 2161–2174a](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2012/89); [Consumer Protection Act, §§ 13 and 19](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/1992/634); [ČOI complaint guidance](https://coi.gov.cz/reklamace/)).

**Launch acceptance implications**

- Provide a withdrawal notice and model form, durable acknowledgement for an online submission, line-level eligibility/reason handling, return evidence, refund timing, and auditable exceptions.
- Provide complaint intake against a durable order line, the required receipt/resolution records and dates, attachments/communications, an explicit agreed extension if any, and deadline alerts/escalation. Keep commercial warranty separate from statutory defect rights.
- Have Legal/Customer Care classify every non-standard product shape (customized/configured, sealed hygiene, perishable, digital content/service) before assigning withdrawal behavior.

## Privacy, cookies, and electronic marketing

GDPR requires a documented purpose and legal basis for each processing operation, transparent information when data are collected, data minimization, storage limitation, data-subject rights, privacy by design/default, processor controls, and security proportionate to risk. Consent is only one legal basis and, where used, must be demonstrable and as easy to withdraw as to give. A privacy notice is not itself consent to processing needed to perform an order or satisfy law. Security must cover confidentiality, integrity, availability, restoration, and regular testing; a qualifying breach can trigger a 72-hour supervisory notification ([GDPR, Articles 5–7, 12–22, 25, 28 and 32–33](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)).

Under current Czech rules, non-essential cookies or comparable device access remain off until prior consent. Necessary storage/access is limited to transmission or a service expressly requested by the user. ÚOOÚ says reject and accept must be equally easy and in the same banner layer, inactivity/closing is not consent, pre-ticked choices are invalid, nontechnical cookies stay disabled before choice, and withdrawal must remain easily available ([Electronic Communications Act 127/2005 Sb., § 89(3)](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2005/127); [ÚOOÚ cookie guidance](https://uoou.gov.cz/verejnost/qa-otazky-a-odpovedi/cookies)).

Electronic marketing normally requires prior, provable consent. The limited existing-customer exception covers the seller's own similar products/services only, requires a clear free opt-out when the contact is collected and in every message, and does not authorize third-party promotion. Each message must be recognizable as commercial, identify whose promotion it is, and offer direct effective refusal. Outsourcing delivery does not remove the commissioning seller's responsibility ([Act 480/2004 Sb., § 7](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2004/480); [ÚOOÚ marketing FAQ](https://uoou.gov.cz/cinnost/obchodni-sdeleni/casto-kladene-otazky-k-zakonu-c-4802004-sb)).

**Launch acceptance implications**

- The Data Protection Owner approves a processing inventory, legal bases, collection-time notices, processor/transfer register, data-subject request flow, retention/deletion matrix, breach ownership, and security evidence before launch.
- Separate required checkout acknowledgements, contract acceptance, optional marketing consent, optional comparison/review programmes, and cookie purposes. Record the notice/consent version, timestamp, actor and later withdrawal; suppress marketing across all senders after opt-out.
- Keep transactional messages purpose-limited: adding product or brand promotion can turn an order/service message into regulated commercial communication.
- Test anonymous, guest Retail Customer, Retail Portal Principal, Counterparty Principal, and assisted-support paths for least data, access control, export/correction/deletion handling, log redaction, backup deletion policy, and absence of optional trackers before consent.

## Tax, invoices, and retention

The Czech Consumer Protection Act requires a purchase document with prescribed seller, date, item/service, and price information on consumer request. VAT-document duties, content, timing, and corrections depend on the seller's VAT status, recipient, supply, and tax treatment; they should therefore be owned by Finance rather than inferred from a universal "invoice for every order" rule ([Consumer Protection Act, § 16](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/1992/634); [VAT Act 235/2004 Sb., §§ 26–35a](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2004/235)).

VAT documents must remain authentic, content-integral, and readable and are generally retained for **10 years from the end of the tax period** in which the supply occurred. Under the current Accounting Act, financial statements/annual reports are generally retained for 10 years and accounting documents, books, depreciation plans, inventories, chart of accounts and overviews for 5 years, with longer preservation where another proceeding or use requires it. GDPR simultaneously forbids retaining identifiable personal data longer than its documented purpose requires; "keep every customer record forever" is not a compliant retention policy ([VAT Act, §§ 34–35a](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2004/235); [Accounting Act 563/1991 Sb., §§ 31–32](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/1991/563); [GDPR, Article 5(1)(e)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)).

**Launch acceptance implications**

- Finance defines which events create a purchase, VAT, corrective, invoice, or credit document; required fields; numbering; handoff/System of Record; electronic-delivery policy; and reconciliation.
- Retention is record-class based, with legal hold, authorized read-only access, integrity/readability tests, export, expiry and evidenced deletion. A historical customer portal is an access view, not the tax/accounting archive itself.
- Legal, Finance, Customer Care, and the Data Protection Owner jointly approve retention for orders, tax/accounting documents, complaints, consent proof, marketing suppression, audit/security logs, and backups. Statutory minima do not automatically authorize exposing all retained data in the customer portal.

## Digital accessibility (EAA and Czech implementation)

Act 424/2023 Sb. implements Directive (EU) 2019/882 and became effective on **28 June 2025**. Covered services provided from **29 June 2025**, including consumer e-commerce through websites or mobile services, must meet the Act's accessibility requirements unless an applicable exception or transition applies. A service-provider microenterprise—fewer than 10 persons and annual turnover **or** annual balance-sheet total no more than EUR 2 million—is exempt. A fundamental-alteration or disproportionate-burden route is narrower than a general product choice and requires a documented assessment, retained for five years and renewed after relevant service changes or at least every five years; the provider must notify the competent authority when relying on it. Pre-existing service contracts may continue unchanged only until expiry and no later than **28 June 2030**; that transition should not be assumed to cover a replacement launch ([Act 424/2023 Sb., §§ 2, 14–16 and 28–29](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2023/424); [MPO applicability explanation](https://mpo.gov.cz/cz/podnikani/pristupnost-vyrobku-a-sluzeb/pristupnost-vyrobku-a-sluzeb-po-ucinnosti-zakona--286983/); [official notification route](https://portal.gov.cz/sluzby-vs/informovani-ceske-obchodni-inspekce-o-neplneni-pozadavku-na-pristupnost-S93683)).

For a covered e-commerce service, the requirements reach the website/mobile service and the path to a consumer contract, including product/service accessibility information supplied by the responsible economic operator and identification, security and payment functions that must be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust. Czech law also requires the service provider to publish accessible information explaining the service and how it meets the requirements, retain compliance information while providing the service, and make it available in audio form on request ([Directive 2019/882, Annex I Sections III–IV](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng); [Act 424/2023 Sb., § 14 and Annex 1](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2023/424); [MPO accessibility-statement guidance](https://mpo.gov.cz/cz/podnikani/pristupnost-vyrobku-a-sluzeb/vzor-prohlaseni-o-pristupnosti--291601/)).

As of **24 March 2026**, MPO reported that harmonized standards supporting a statutory presumption of conformity were still unavailable. Its e-commerce guide maps practical testing to WCAG 2.2 and EN 301 549, but MPO expressly says the guides themselves do not create a presumption of conformity. Therefore "WCAG 2.2 AA" can be a sensible engineering acceptance baseline, but should not be recorded as automatic legal sign-off ([MPO status and guidance](https://mpo.gov.cz/cz/podnikani/pristupnost-vyrobku-a-sluzeb/navodne-dokumenty-k-zajisteni-pozadavku-na-pristupnost--292236/); [MPO e-commerce guide](https://mpo.gov.cz/assets/cz/podnikani/pristupnost-vyrobku-a-sluzeb/2026/3/Navodny-dokument-D-_-Elektronicke-obchodovani-_e_commerce_.pdf)).

**Launch acceptance implications**

- First obtain a named Legal/Executive decision on Akros's service-provider identity, consumer e-commerce scope, employee/financial thresholds, and any claimed exception. Preserve the assessment; do not treat an unknown company size as an exemption.
- Unless an approved exemption applies, make accessibility a launch gate across B2C discovery, configuration, cart, checkout, authentication, payment handoff/return, order documents, withdrawal/complaint forms, customer support, and the published accessibility information. Include keyboard and assistive-technology use, text alternatives, structure/labels, focus, errors, status changes, reflow/zoom/contrast, and accessible documents in automated plus representative manual testing.
- Include third-party checkout, payment, identity, consent, chat, document and media components in the assessment wherever Akros finances, creates, or can influence them. Record known limitations, remediation ownership, user contact/escalation, and regression evidence.

## Conditional age-restricted-goods gate

The deployed legacy 18+ marker does not establish that Akros currently sells a restricted category or that a checkbox would satisfy the category's law. The Product/Legal owners must inventory the launch catalogue and identify every age- or licence-restricted class before deciding whether an age-control capability is in scope. For example, Czech law permits distance sale of alcohol only where sale to under-18s is excluded and requires a conspicuous pre-sale warning; tobacco, nicotine and related products have their own more detailed remote-sale and age-verification rules ([Act 65/2017 Sb., §§ 6–7 and 15–16c](https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2017/65); [ČOI enforcement summary](https://coi.gov.cz/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/COI-vyrocni-zprava-2024.pdf)).

If no restricted goods are accepted for launch, retire the generic 18+ checkout mechanism and record the catalogue prohibition. If any are accepted, the category owner must supply the exact eligibility, warning, identity/age verification, delivery handoff, record, privacy and refusal rules; acceptance must prove that a minor cannot complete the purchase, not just that a checkbox is present.

## Near-term legal watch for a launch after this note

Two changes are close enough that a future launch cannot rely on this **2026-08-22** snapshot without re-checking enacted Czech law:

- Directive (EU) 2024/825 requires its consumer green-transition rules to apply from **27 September 2026**. As of the Czech Parliament's **6 August 2026** update, implementing Bill 53 had completed only second reading. The expected rules constrain unsupported generic environmental claims, offset-only climate-neutral claims, non-certification sustainability labels, and certain future-performance claims. Until the Czech text is enacted, do not treat draft wording as law; avoid designing unsupported eco claims/badges and make Legal re-check the enacted text immediately before sign-off ([Directive (EU) 2024/825, Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/825/oj/eng); [Czech Bill 53 tracker](https://www.psp.cz/sqw/historie.sqw?o=10&t=53); [MPO status and guidance](https://mpo.gov.cz/cz/ochrana-spotrebitele/prehledne-o-ochrane-spotrebitele/nova-pravidla-_-environmentalni-tvrzeni-a-info-povinnosti--293928/)).
- MPO reported on **9 July 2026** that a pending Czech amendment intended for **27 September 2026** would introduce prescribed harmonized consumer notices about statutory defect rights and, where supplied for a qualifying whole-product manufacturer durability guarantee, a product-specific online label under Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1960. Because the Czech amendment was still pending, treat this as a launch watch item and content-model extension point, not a currently binding Akros requirement ([MPO status](https://mpo.gov.cz/cz/ochrana-spotrebitele/prehledne-o-ochrane-spotrebitele/harmonizovane-informace-o-zarukach--291656/); [Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1960](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1960/oj/eng)).

## Required owner confirmations and sign-off

Before the cutline can be called production-complete, record named approvals for:

1. **Legal/Consumer Owner:** seller identity; B2C versus B2B contract boundary; terms; pre-contract/final-step content; withdrawal exceptions; complaint remedies and ADR text; product-safety applicability; restricted-goods catalogue; and any accessibility exception.
2. **Finance/Tax Owner:** VAT status and supply rules; price/tax/fee presentation; discount classifications; document triggers/content/timing; corrective documents; System of Record; reconciliation; and statutory retention.
3. **Data Protection Owner:** controller/processors; purposes and legal bases; notices; cookies; marketing programmes; data-subject rights; transfers; security/breach ownership; consent evidence; and the retention/deletion matrix.
4. **Accessibility Owner plus accountable executive:** scoped service/components; selected measurable engineering baseline; manual testing with representative assistive technologies; supplier evidence; accessibility information and contact route; known exceptions/limitations; remediation; and release sign-off.
5. **Customer Care/Operations Owner:** durable confirmations; cancellation/withdrawal/return/refund and complaint workflows; 14-/30-day timers; document availability; legal holds; exception ownership; and audit evidence.

These are launch gates for the accepted Czech B2C+B2B promise. Features classified `later`, `archive/read-only`, or `retire` still need the same legal screening when picked up; postponement does not convert a legal requirement on an already launched journey into optional scope.

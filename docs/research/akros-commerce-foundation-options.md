# Akros commerce-foundation options

> **Historical evidence notice (superseded 2026-08-23):** This artifact preserves the alternatives considered before the binding OntOS product correction. Its sibling-product, separately shared Core, engine-derived foundation, and modular-monolith recommendations are rejected. The current decision is the clean [Commerce Application Composition inside OntOS](../product/akros-commerce-foundation.md). Treat engine-specific material below only as behavior and delivery-risk evidence, never target runtime, source, naming, or compatibility guidance.

Research for **[Choose the commerce foundation for production Akros](https://github.com/TSNheathen/wayrepo/issues/9)**. This is decision evidence at business-roadmap depth, not an implementation design.

## Decision frame

The foundation must reach the accepted Czech B2C/B2B launch cutline, including contextual commercial terms, durable Orders, payment and Fulfillment recovery, Claims, migration of open obligations, governed Commerce Operations, and production proof—not generic feature breadth or WRShop parity (`../product/akros-product-cutline.md:7-14,23-90`). OntOS terminology and architecture have highest authority: Akros and OntOS are sibling products, Core owns cross-cutting guarantees, commerce domains own commerce behavior, and sharing must not delay production Akros (`../product/akros-product-domain-boundaries.md:5-23,38-50`).

The accepted integration decision is also a hard gate. Commerce-foundation placement may vary, but externally caused writes must remain governed Actions; confirmed Orders and outbound handoffs must be durable; exact-result idempotency, reconciliation, audited recovery, and one declared System of Record per fact or transition cannot be weakened (`../product/akros-data-authority-integration-contracts.md:65-88`).

The product-owner preference supplied during this decision is to optimize for production Akros and **own Akros contracts and extensions, not the commerce-engine source**. Full source internalization is a later option only if operating evidence proves it worthwhile. This rules out treating a framework fork as the default route to ownership.

Primary repositories were inspected at OntOS commit `1bb66855b02a54108e82ca1036f9426e94c6d24f` and first-party `new-engine` commit `0db98293608f3f615318ff61f88c3d086e753de1`. Official framework documentation was checked on 2026-08-23. Documentation establishes supported contracts, not Akros production performance.

## What the evidence establishes

### OntOS is a credible authority kernel, not a commerce implementation

OntOS deliberately separates Core guarantees from module-owned business meaning. It specifies registered Actions, command-owned canonical writes, audit/events/outbox in Postgres, and asynchronous workers; Core must not absorb business policy merely because two domains look similar (`../context/ontos/03_ARCHITECTURE_OVERVIEW.md:5-33`; `../context/ontos/06_CORE_KERNEL.md:29-51`). This is the required architectural direction for every option.

The current implementation cannot by itself reach the Akros cutline. Its installed-module registry contains only `ticketing`, while current Core documentation explicitly leaves concrete business Actions, full manifest generation, and generic exact-response idempotency replay out of scope (`../context/ontos/app/packages/shared-contracts/src/module-state.ts:1-24`; `../context/ontos/app/packages/core-runtime/docs/action-ecosystem.md:65-87`). Exact replay is already an accepted Akros integration guarantee, so Core hardening is prerequisite under every option—not evidence for rebuilding all commerce natively now.

The OntOS modular-monolith ADR says the team is small, the timeline constrained, and the domain still being discovered (`../context/ontos/adr/0002-modular-monolith-for-v0.md:3-15`). Although that ADR remains proposed, its capacity premise strongly disfavors simultaneously productizing Core and recreating the complete commerce engine and Commerce Operations surface.

### `new-engine` is the only current first-party commerce implementation evidence

The inspected code is a Medusa v2 commerce monorepo, not an Akros application (`../../context/new-engine/AGENTS.md:5-34`). Its backend currently pins the Medusa 2.18 family and exposes unit, module-integration, HTTP end-to-end, and Admin end-to-end test commands (`../../context/new-engine/apps/medusa-be/package.json:15-41,62-80`). The configured commerce surface includes product enrichment, lists, reviews, Claims, company/quote/approval behavior, Order extensions, payment-return state, market authority, payment and Fulfillment providers, Content, Symmy, search, and an order dashboard (`../../context/new-engine/apps/medusa-be/src/config/modules.ts:214-318`; `../../context/new-engine/apps/medusa-be/src/config/plugins.ts:5-29`). CI separately exercises backend unit, module-integration, and HTTP end-to-end suites (`../../context/new-engine/.github/workflows/medusa-be-tests-after-ci.yml:109-187`).

The evidence is especially relevant to Akros's B2C/B2B and Commerce Operations cutline: the storefront supports retail/wholesale registration (`../../context/new-engine/apps/herbatika/src/lib/auth/auth-form-validators.ts:25-47,64-65,149-166`), while the internal dashboard represents payment, Fulfillment, carrier-label, exception-queue, filtering, and guarded status-change concerns (`../../context/new-engine/apps/medusa-order-dashboard-plugin/src/admin/routes/order-dashboard/i18n.ts:1-77,79-125,176-225`).

This is strong evidence of implemented behavior and team familiarity, but weak evidence of direct Akros fit. The concrete production composition and four-market readiness work concern Herbatika, not Akros (`../../context/new-engine/README.md:82-90`; `../../context/new-engine/apps/herbatika/tests/market-readiness/gate.node-test.mjs:575-601`). No inspected source proves current Akros traffic, capacity, open-obligation migration, exact cutline parity, or OntOS-governed writes.

### Established engines supply generic maturity, not local parity

Medusa's current official architecture provides pre-built commerce modules, workflows, PostgreSQL persistence, replaceable infrastructure providers, Admin APIs/extensions, and production-oriented Redis/S3 providers ([architecture](https://docs.medusajs.com/learn/introduction/architecture), [modules](https://docs.medusajs.com/learn/fundamentals/modules), [plugins](https://docs.medusajs.com/learn/fundamentals/plugins), [deployment](https://docs.medusajs.com/learn/deployment/general)). This corroborates the framework seams visible in `new-engine`; it does not prove that an unmodified Medusa lifecycle satisfies OntOS governance. Medusa also permits breaking changes in minor releases, making version pinning, upgrade evidence, and rollback capacity ongoing ownership duties ([updating Medusa](https://docs.medusajs.com/learn/update)). Its repository license identifies RBAC and SSO as proprietary Enterprise materials, so Akros must either rely on OntOS authorization/identity or explicitly accept a commercial dependency rather than assuming the stock Admin meets governed Commerce Operations needs ([Enterprise license](https://github.com/medusajs/medusa/blob/develop/ENTERPRISE-LICENSE.md)).

Vendure is a reasonable high-fit exemplar for the “another established engine” category because it is also TypeScript-based and provides separate Storefront/Admin APIs, an extensible Dashboard, plugins for owned domain logic and integrations, and a worker/job-queue model ([architecture](https://docs.vendure.io/current/core/developer-guide/overview), [plugins](https://docs.vendure.io/current/core/developer-guide/plugins), [worker and job queue](https://docs.vendure.io/current/core/developer-guide/worker-job-queue), [deployment](https://docs.vendure.io/current/core/deployment)). Saleor provides a contrasting external-App and webhook extension model ([extension model](https://docs.saleor.io/developer/extending/overview)). These establish a plausible category, not an Akros selection. Vendure's GPL/commercial licensing also needs legal review ([license](https://github.com/vendurehq/vendure/blob/master/LICENSE.md)). There is no first-party Akros/Vendure or Akros/Saleor behavior, extension, migration, operator, or team-operability evidence comparable to `new-engine`.

## Recommendation-ready route and gates

Choose the **deliberately staged combination**. Use the current Medusa-derived implementation as the initial commerce behavior source and compatibility asset, while the OntOS-derived Core remains the sole authority for shared runtime guarantees. Own provider-neutral Akros contracts and extensions; keep the engine replaceable and externally maintained. This is not approval to deploy Herbatika unchanged, fork/internalize Medusa, or make Medusa a second architecture.

At roadmap depth, the stages are:

1. **Characterize before committing:** express the accepted Akros launch journeys and failure/recovery cases as provider-neutral behavioral acceptance tests; measure `new-engine` against them and record gaps. Existing Herbatika behavior counts only where the Akros outcome and canonical ownership match.
2. **Establish the governed seam:** stable Storefront and Commerce Operations contracts, one writer per aggregate, and the accepted Action, policy, audit, outbox, exact-replay, Connector, and reconciliation guarantees are launch gates. The later application-shape and consistency decisions determine physical placement; this decision does not authorize dual writes or nested cross-runtime transactions.
3. **Launch through the smallest proven compatibility scope:** retain Medusa-derived behavior only for commerce lifecycles it demonstrably satisfies. Preserve the current operator surface where useful, but route every state change through domain-owned, permissioned behavior.
4. **Move ownership only by evidence:** a native commerce module replaces a Medusa-owned capability only after behavioral parity, migration/reconciliation, Commerce Operations, rollback, and production acceptance pass. Low-risk reads and projections may move before Cart, Inventory reservation, Order, Payment/refund, or Fulfillment writes.

The staged choice should be reconsidered if any gate fails:

- the Medusa-derived behavior cannot meet a launch-critical Akros rule without violating OntOS ownership or consistency;
- Core cannot provide exact replay and the other accepted runtime guarantees without unsafe dual ownership;
- the combined operational burden exceeds named team capacity; or
- a bounded alternative-engine prototype demonstrates a material launch-critical advantage after including migration, owned extensions, Commerce Operations, and rollback—not merely feature-list breadth.

## Remaining uncertainty

- No primary evidence yet measures Akros parity against the accepted cutline, current production traffic, peak throughput, failure rates, or operator load.
- No migration inventory establishes the volume and lifecycle state of open Orders, payments, shipments, Claims, Principal bindings, documents, or retained history.
- No prototype proves the safest physical seam between Medusa-derived behavior and CoreSDK governance, especially for atomic writes and exact-result replay.
- The team is described as small, but current staffing, Medusa/OntOS expertise distribution, support capacity, and delivery dates are unverified.
- Official framework documentation establishes supported mechanisms, not comparative reliability in the Akros workload. Vendure is only a representative credible alternative; no alternative-engine selection has been performed.

These uncertainties affect execution gates and sequencing, but they do not overturn the current evidence ranking: a native-first rewrite has too much unproven scope; changing engines discards the strongest local asset without a demonstrated gain; a permanent Medusa-only target conflicts with OntOS authority; staged compatibility preserves the most behavior and the most reversibility.
